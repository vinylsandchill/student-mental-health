Green · Early awareness and prevention

Checking in with myself

You don't need to be in crisis to take your wellbeing seriously.

Changes can look different for everyone. Something that has been difficult for a long time still deserves support.

Changes worth noticing

  • Changes in mood: Feeling more anxious, irritable, low, or emotionally exhausted than usual. You might notice that small challenges feel harder to manage.
  • Changes in behaviour: Withdrawing from friends, avoiding activities you usually enjoy, or relying more heavily on coping behaviours that concern you.
  • Changes in everyday functioning: Finding it harder to attend lectures, complete coursework, maintain personal hygiene, prepare meals, or manage your usual responsibilities.
  • Changes in physical wellbeing: Difficulty sleeping, changes in appetite, persistent tiredness, or difficulty concentrating.

These signs don't automatically mean a crisis is coming. What matters is how you're feeling and whether you could use support.

Small steps you can try

Build a sustainable routine

Having a few regular activities can help create structure during university life. Try choosing consistent times for meals, sleep, or taking a break from studying.

Start with one manageable habit rather than trying to change your whole routine at once.

Check your occupational balance

University life can involve competing demands. Think about how much time you spend studying, socialising, resting, looking after yourself, and doing activities you enjoy.

Is anything taking up too much of your time? Is there something meaningful you've stopped making time for? Consider one small adjustment that could improve your balance.

Identify what helps you feel well

Think about activities, environments, and people that have a positive effect on your wellbeing. These might include spending time outdoors, exercising, creative hobbies, or seeing friends.

Choose one activity you enjoy and make some time for it this week.

Create a personal wellbeing plan

Write down a few things you can refer to when life becomes more difficult:

  • Changes that might indicate you're struggling.
  • Activities that usually support your wellbeing.
  • People you feel comfortable reaching out to.
  • Support services you could contact if needed.

Keep your plan somewhere easy to access, such as your phone's notes app.

Want some extra support?

It is okay to ask early. University wellbeing services and your GP can help, even if you're unsure what you're experiencing.
