Red · Urgent and emergency support

I need urgent help

You deserve support. You do not have to work out the perfect words before asking for help.

I'm in immediate danger or can't stay safe

Call 999 now or go to your nearest A&E. This includes when someone's life is at risk, someone has seriously harmed themselves, or you cannot keep yourself or another person safe.

If possible, ask someone you trust to help you contact emergency services or accompany you to A&E.

Do not drive yourself to A&E. Ask someone to take you, or call 999 if you need an ambulance.

I need urgent help, but I'm not in immediate danger

In England, call NHS 111 and select the mental health option. You can ask for urgent mental health advice and support.

If you're unsure how urgent things are, NHS 111 can advise you. If immediate danger develops, call 999.

I need someone to talk to

Samaritans

Call 116 123 free, any time, day or night, to talk about what's troubling you.

Shout

Text SHOUT to 85258 for free, 24/7 text-based support in the UK.

Listening services are not a replacement for emergency medical help when someone is in immediate danger.

What happens when I ask for help?

Calling NHS 111

In England, you can select the mental health option when calling NHS 111. You'll be asked about what you're experiencing so the service can help you find appropriate support.

Calling 999 or going to A&E

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, emergency services can assess the situation and arrange appropriate care. At A&E, healthcare professionals can assess your immediate physical and mental health needs.

Contacting a listening service

Samaritans and Shout offer confidential, non-judgemental support. You can talk about what's troubling you without needing to explain everything perfectly.

What can I say?

“I'm having a mental health crisis and need help. I'm at [your location]. I'm worried I can't keep myself safe.”

It's okay to read this from your screen or ask someone else to speak for you.

While waiting for help

  • Move somewhere safer if you can.
  • Contact a trusted person who can stay with you or help you get support.
  • Follow instructions from the service you've contacted.
  • If the situation becomes immediately dangerous, call 999.
