Amber · Practical steps and additional support

I'm finding it hard to cope

When things feel difficult, you don't need to solve everything at once. Try one step that feels manageable and consider reaching out for support.

First, check what help you need

If you need urgent mental health help, contact NHS 111 and select the mental health option in England. If you or someone else is in immediate danger or cannot stay safe, call 999 or go to A&E.

See urgent help guidance →

Getting additional support

University wellbeing services may help with emotional support and signposting. A GP can discuss mental health concerns and treatment options. If coursework or attendance is affected, ask about academic adjustments or disability support.

For someone to talk to now, call Samaritans on 116 123, or text SHOUT to 85258 (UK).

Small steps you can try now

Feel more grounded

Notice five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear, two you can smell, and one you can taste.

You can adapt this exercise to suit your needs, or stop if it doesn't feel helpful.

Reduce immediate demands

When everything feels overwhelming, you don't have to tackle every responsibility at once. Identify what genuinely needs your attention today and what can wait.

Break essential tasks into smaller steps. For example, opening a document or sending one email can be enough for now.

Adjust your environment

Your surroundings can affect how manageable everyday activities feel. If possible, move somewhere quieter, reduce bright lighting, put on comfortable clothing, or take a break from a crowded environment.

Think about what would make your immediate surroundings feel more comfortable and manageable.

Reach out for support

You don't need to manage everything alone. Consider contacting a trusted friend, family member, university wellbeing service, or GP.

If explaining how you feel is difficult, you could start with: "I'm having a difficult time and could use some support. Could we talk?"
