How to find support at your university

  1. Visit your university website and search for student wellbeing, mental health support, or student services.
  2. Look for ways to book an appointment or contact the team by email or phone.
  3. If studying is affected, also search for disability support, academic adjustments, or extenuating circumstances.
  4. If you aren't sure which service to use, contact your student support team or personal tutor and ask where to start.

Not sure what to say?

You don't need to explain everything in your first message. You can start by asking what support is available.

For example:

I'm finding things difficult at the moment, and it's starting to affect my university experience. I'd like to know what support is available and how I can access it.

If contacting someone feels overwhelming, you could ask a trusted person to help you write the message or find the right service.

Healthcare support

Your GP can discuss mental health difficulties and help you explore treatment or referrals. In England, you can also explore NHS Talking Therapies ↗ for eligible concerns.

Support should fit your life

Everyone's circumstances are different. Disability, financial pressures, caring responsibilities, cultural differences, or limited time can make accessing support more difficult.

You deserve support that considers your individual needs. You can ask services about accessible appointments, reasonable adjustments, online consultations, or alternative ways to communicate.

Support should help you participate in the everyday activities that matter to you, whether that's studying, working, socialising, or looking after yourself.
